Book I

The state and its doctrine

The full constitutional description of Hrossagrið is a eudaimonocratic diarchic monarchy. Eudaimonocracy answers why state power exists. The diarchy answers who holds it.

A human state. Hrossagrið is governed by and for humans. Its citizens, monarchs, officials, voters, workers, patients and passengers are people. Horses and other equids are protected living beings and working partners; they are not the population and hold no human political office.

Eudaimonocracy

Article I · The purpose of state power

The name comes from eudaimonia, the classical Greek idea usually mistranslated as happiness. It is closer to flourishing: a life going well as a whole, rather than a moment that feels good. A person can enjoy something while destroying their own life, and something unpleasant today can be necessary for a genuinely good life overall.

From that comes the Hrossagrithic term: government oriented toward flourishing. But the Empire does not simply copy Aristotle. Classical eudaimonia is primarily about human flourishing, and Hrossagrið deliberately widens the frame using an existing eight-part ethical scheme.

The legitimate purpose of state power is to maximise constructive survival and flourishing and minimise serious destruction across the Eight Dynamics, using the best available evidence to determine the real consequences of policy.

The Eight Dynamics

Article II · The ethical frame, and where it comes from

The Eight Dynamics are not a Hrossagrithic invention. They are a concept from Scientology, which describes the basic principle of existence as a drive toward survival divided into eight interconnected urges. The Empire adopts that framework as its ethical scheme; the political conclusions drawn from it are its own, and the standing of the religion it comes from is set out in the article that follows.

  1. Self — the urge to survive as an individual and to be oneself.
  2. Creativity — making things for the future: sex, family, children, raising them, creation directed forward.
  3. Groups — survival through or as a group of any scale, from a school class to a state.
  4. Mankind — the human species as a whole, distinct from any one nation.
  5. Life Forms — animals, birds, insects, fish and vegetation; life itself.
  6. Physical Universe — matter, energy, space and time.
  7. Spiritual Dynamic — spiritual beings, spiritual existence, and the spiritual source of life.
  8. Infinity — existence as infinity, associated with God, the Supreme Being or the Creator.

In the source framework, actions that increase survival across the majority of the Dynamics are regarded as constructive, and actions that destroy or deny them as destructive. That is the part Hrossagrið turns into a political method.

The frame does not supply an arithmetic. It settles what must be weighed, not how a real conflict between the Dynamics is to be resolved, and the Empire has no settled doctrine for that balancing. Reducing the eight to a single national score is rejected; how a genuine conflict is to be decided remains an open question rather than a solved one.

Nor does every law in force follow from the frame plus the evidence. The prohibition on ordinary motor road transport is, at least in origin, a cultural preference rather than a conclusion derived from welfare evidence, and the Empire is not accurately described as having no preference-based laws at all.

Scientology and the state

Article III · Influence without authority

Scientology may be the predominant religion of Hrossagrið and a major source of the Empire’s ethical philosophy. Its cultural influence is great, and its influence on the private life of the royal houses is greater still.

It is not compulsory, and it governs nothing. No person may be forced to become a Scientologist. There is no religious test for citizenship, education, welfare or employment, Church office confers no civil authority of any kind, and the Church of Scientology does not govern Hrossagrið — the Crowns govern the state. A citizen may disagree with Scientology, reject it, leave it, follow another religion or none, and criticise it publicly, without penalty of any kind. The Empire may believe something strongly without requiring every person in it to believe the same.

What eudaimonocracy is not

Article IV · Four standing misreadings

  • Not a happiness machine. Immediate pleasure is not eudaimonia. A policy is judged by its effect on a life over time, and on families, groups and others.
  • Not ordinary democracy. There is a Parliament, elections, public opinion and the right to criticise government — but the majority is not the absolute sovereign. If the evidence strongly indicates a course will cause enormous harm, the answer can be no. Fifty-one per cent of votes does not turn a destructive act into a constructive one.
  • Not technocracy. Experts establish and test facts. Whether an outcome is wanted, and at what cost, is a political and ethical question; a scientist does not become monarch by being a scientist.
  • Not simple utilitarianism. The question is not which total of benefit is larger, but what exactly is being created and destroyed, where, how seriously, and whether the same result can be reached with less destruction.

The Imperial Diarchy

Article V · Two Crowns, equal in authority

Hrossagrið is governed through two permanent and separate offices, equal in governing authority. Neither monarch commands, outranks, or may unilaterally dismiss the other. The holder of either Crown may use the title Empress or Emperor; the diarchy may consist of two Empresses, two Emperors, or one of each.

Herd archetypes The two Crowns are understood culturally through the guiding lead mare, who determines direction, and the protective stallion, who scouts and guards. These are symbols of function — not constitutional rules about sex or gender, and no restriction on any citizen.

  • The Guiding Crown

    Domestic government, diplomacy, civil administration, economic and social direction, infrastructure, and the Empire’s long-term course.

  • The Guarding Crown

    Defence, borders, national security, military readiness, protective intelligence, emergency protection and response, scouting, and protective strategy.

The Guiding Crown has seniority only in ceremonial precedence, and where the same person would simultaneously become next entitled to a lawful offer from both succession lines. That seniority grants no greater governing power.

There is one state, one government, one cabinet, one national budget, one military structure and one civil service. Neither Crown may create a rival government, treasury, military, intelligence service or administration, issue orders through the other’s chain of command, or dismiss the other’s officials.

What requires both Crowns

Each Crown decides ordinary matters whose predominant purpose and direct legal effect lie in its own field. A decision becomes joint when it directly alters the other Crown’s legal authority or chain of command; commits or removes a substantial share of shared money, personnel, infrastructure or core operational capacity; creates a binding national obligation in both fields; or creates a concrete, substantial and reasonably probable effect on the other Crown’s safety or ability to perform its duties.

Actual effect controls. A decision cannot escape joint approval by being divided into smaller acts, or relabelled temporary, experimental, administrative, secret, military or emergency-adjacent. Disputes over classification — not over policy — go to three senior judges drawn by public audited lot.

Deadlock

When the Crowns disagree on a joint matter, neither may impose the disputed proposal. An ordinary joint proposal must be resolved within thirty days, an urgent one within seventy-two hours; if agreement is not reached, the proposal fails. Silence, refusal to attend or delay never counts as approval.

The previous lawful position then continues — public services, defensive readiness, necessary temporary appointments, and an essential budget adjusted only by published objective formula. This exists so that neither a Crown nor Parliament can manufacture a collapse by running out the clock.

Emergency action

During an active or imminent attack, disaster or other time-critical threat, the Guarding Crown may issue immediate operational orders within its field without waiting for joint approval. An urgent threat inside the Guiding field — epidemic, failure of food, water or infrastructure, seizure of the financial system — permits the Guiding Crown the same minimum temporary orders. Neither may amend the Constitution, succession or the basic distribution of power, and neither may disguise an ordinary policy preference as an emergency. Every emergency order is recorded with its evidence and reviewed at least every seventy-two hours.

Parliament and the courts

Article VI · Crown supremacy inside the constitution

Hrossagrið is Crown-dominant, but Crown supremacy exists within the Constitution rather than outside it. Parliament represents the public, debates and drafts laws, investigates misconduct, scrutinises administration and administers delegated powers. It cannot enact law over the refusal of the Crown or Crowns whose assent is required, and it has no override.

Succession, regency and removal

Article VII · Two lines, kept separate

The two Crowns have separate succession lines, and no will, inheritance, marriage, adoption, emergency or election may exchange or merge them. Either Crown is filled in a fixed order: first a valid testamentary appointment made by the previous holder of that same Crown; failing that, the hereditary line of that Crown; and only where neither produces a holder, a national election held specifically for that Crown. Acceptance and refusal are real: no one is bound to accept. A minor monarch governs through a regency council with authority released progressively by age, and temporary incapacity, disappearance, medical retirement, abdication and removal each have their own defined procedure.

Election without candidates

An imperial election is deliberately unlike a campaign. Candidates stand as anonymous code-holders. The public receives a random candidate code, an anonymous eligibility certificate and one standardised written statement of the candidate’s morals, ethics, anonymised experience, governing outlook and what they would attempt in the Crown sought — and no name, age, appearance, voice, sex, family, home region, employer or other identifying detail. Public debates, private and third-party advertising, donations, endorsements, polling and targeted promotion are prohibited; only the Election Commission distributes candidate material, equally and at public expense. Deliberate self-identification disqualifies the candidate. The winner’s identity is revealed as part of the public registration of the final accession, and never earlier.

Every ballot carries the candidate codes and a Reject All option, and a candidate wins only with more than half of all valid ballots, Reject All included. If Reject All takes that majority, every candidate of that round is barred from standing again for the same vacancy; nominations reopen and a fresh ballot is held.

Candidates are expected to state intentions rather than promises, because an explicit promise is legally binding. A candidate who insists on making one must define the promised result, its measurable completion, its intermediate milestones and the earliest achievable deadline, and the Commission removes any candidate insisting on a promise that is unlawful, impossible or outside the authority of the Crown sought. After accession the monarch publishes an implementation schedule and annual progress reports. Unforeseeable impossibility, found by judges drawn by lot, releases the monarch without penalty; a knowingly impossible promise, a concealed barrier or a culpably missed deadline is grave constitutional fraud, and the consequences extend to removal.

Foundational values

Article VIII · Eight, in the order recorded

  • Historical honesty
  • Meritocracy
  • Animal welfare
  • Scientific progress
  • Environmental stewardship
  • Refugee integration
  • Social equality
  • Ethical governance

The corresponding body of binding instruments — codes, technical standards and official templates — is catalogued in the register of imperial law. What the state provides for the people who live here is set out in Living in Hrossagrið.

Thresholds Where the Constitution sets a parliamentary threshold, it is measured against full statutory membership. Vacancies, absences and abstentions do not lower the denominator.

Lots Judges and auditors for constitutional disputes are drawn by public audited lot from frozen professional rosters maintained by permanent civil servants.
