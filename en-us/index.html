Entry I · The founding meaning

Why the Empire carries this name

Recorded in the imperial register

Hrossagrið is not named for its horses. It is named for its grið — guaranteed peace, safe conduct, quarter, sanctuary — and for the horses that define whom the sanctuary is owed to. The name was not chosen. It was given, in the Viking Age, by Norse speakers who found a law that granted horses protected peace ridiculous.

They had two words for it. Hrossaþrælar — horse-thralls — was thrown at the people. Hrossagrið mocked what their law did: that place where even a horse receives legal peace. The insult aimed at people died with the quarrel. The one that named a jurisdiction did not: outsiders who had never been in the quarrel heard only a plain description — the country that protects horses — and used it as such. The Hrossagrith knew exactly where it came from and took it anyway.

Note in the margin The charge was that these were people who had put an animal under the protection of the law. The Empire has never disputed the charge. See how the name was given.

Protection of a person is incomplete where the person’s horse, means of movement, livelihood, or route home may still be seized, abused, exhausted, or used as leverage.

Entry III · The state

What the Empire is

Population, constitution and character

Modern Hrossagrið is a human nation, and a highly diverse one: its population descends from numerous peoples integrated over centuries through migration and refuge. Horses and other equids are protected living beings, companions and working partners, and the foundation of the Empire’s road-transport culture. They are not the population, and they hold no human political office.

Constitutionally, the Empire is a eudaimonocratic diarchic monarchy: two co-equal Crowns, the Guiding and the Guarding, governing under a philosophy that measures a policy by what it builds and what it destroys across the whole breadth of life rather than by what a majority happens to want.

Historical truth. The Empire does not rewrite, erase, or sanitize its history for political purposes. Its record of its own successes, failures, injustices and controversial decisions is meant to be accurate, and the history section states the limits of its own reconstruction rather than hiding them.
