National treasure

Horses

Every other part of this portal could be explained without mentioning horses. None of it would exist in this shape without them.

The Empire is named for the protection owed to horses, and that protection is the oldest thing in its law. What follows is not sentiment: it is the reasoning by which the Empire decides what may and may not be done to an animal, and the marks by which its road system makes that decision visible.

Scope Hrossagrithic law covers ponies, donkeys, mules, hinnies and other equids. The ancient national name keeps the horse only as its representative root.

Article I

Welfare and bitless control

Why ordinary bits are prohibited

A bit is an object placed in a horse’s mouth and connected to the reins, so that the rider or driver controls the animal by pressure through the mouth. In most of the horse world this is unremarkable. In Hrossagrið ordinary bits are simply banned.

Because the horse counts as a life form in its own right rather than as somebody’s property, the analysis cannot end at what is convenient for the rider. The state has to ask what happens to the horse, whether pain is risked, whether a less destructive alternative exists, and whether the animal can be controlled safely without a device in its mouth. Where the answer is yes, human convenience is not enough to justify the additional risk.

Approved bitless headgear includes sidepulls, hackamores, bosals, cross-unders and cordeos. What is lawful is set by the Imperial Tack, Restraint and Welfare Code and measured against the Imperial Tack Measurement Schedule; road use adds further requirements on top.

Slaughter. Hrossagrithic culture and law treat equids far closer to companion animals and working partners than to food animals. Other societies have long traditions of eating horse meat; the Empire does not regard that as a reason to tolerate an equid-meat industry. Horse slaughter is one of the gravest equid-welfare crimes — and the human offender still receives the highest available non-lethal penalties, because the death penalty is absolutely prohibited.

Older than the doctrine The prohibition is not a deduction from the Empire’s later political philosophy. It came out of horse-handling practice and the evidence on equine welfare, and the doctrine was written afterwards.

Article II

Road status at a glance

The mark every equid carries onto a public road

Every equid entering a public road wears approved bitless headgear fitted with a Facial Marker — a marker-bearing frentera or muserola carrying an interchangeable status insert, readable from the front. The marker is a road instruction addressed to humans. It is not decoration, not advertising, and not a substitute for the judgment of the rider or driver.

Color is never the only distinction. Every status also carries its own border, shape or symbol, so that the system stays readable to color-blind road users.

Ordinary and restricted statuses

  • White field Ordinary road-certified equid.
  • Green field Green equid, provisional certificate, training status, or supervised road work.
  • Yellow field Caution: sensitivity, extra-space requirement, or heightened startle risk.
  • White field, red border Restricted passing. Pass only on the signal of the driver, rider or handler.
  • Red field, white X Do not pass. Stop and wait: an active hazard or immediate control condition exists.

Service statuses

  • Blue, Road Guard emblem Imperial Road Guard, traffic patrol, road-control service.
  • Blue, police shield Police service, where authorized.
  • White, red cross Ambulance or authorized medical emergency service.
  • Red, white flame Fire or rescue service.
  • Green, paw and hoof Veterinary emergency service.
  • Blue, pegasus Imperial post, messenger or courier service.
  • Black, white border Sovereign passage, where legally authorized.
  • Black with crown Authorized imperial government, ministerial or state authority passage.
  • Dark green, sigil Military service.

These drawings show the distinguishing field, border and symbol only. Exact color values and tolerances are published by the competent road authority and verified with calibrated measurement.

Article III

Service equids

Selection by function, not by fashion

Police, ambulance, taxi, courier, veterinary, fire, military and sovereign service equids are selected on what they can actually do: endurance, recovery, road courage, temperament, traffic sanity, the ability to stand still and to move fast, tolerance of crowds and lights, and lawful bitless control.

They are not selected for being purebred, flashy, fashionable or dramatic. A functional head and airway matter more than an exaggerated profile, and individual certification overrides any breed label. Ridden service is governed separately from carriage work, under the Imperial Mounted Service and Road Patrol Code.

Where the rules live General Equid Welfare Code · Tack, Restraint and Welfare Code · Carriage Equids Code · Mounted Service and Road Patrol Code.
