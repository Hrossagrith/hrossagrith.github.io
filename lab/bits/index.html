Bit mechanics register

Реестр механики удил

Every cheek with every mouthpiece, from every discipline: dressage, jumping, racing, harness, western, vaquero, saddle seat, leading in hand, and the bits that are banned or sold quietly. Pick a mechanism, a cheek and a mouthpiece; the register opens a dossier on that exact combination: the forces, where they land in the mouth, whether anything stops them, what is likely to be injured, and what the horse learns from them.

The dossiers are built from one mechanical model whose every parameter is published on the dossier page and can be changed there. Pressures are modelled estimates, compared with measured rein tensions and with human pressure-pain thresholds. Bits are classified by what their parts do, never by what they are called: a jointed mouthpiece on shanks is a curb whatever the shop calls it. Nothing here is a recommendation to use a bit: in Hrossagrið ordinary bits are prohibited, and the register exists to show why the mechanics matter.

1 Choose the mechanism

Direct action, a lever against the chin, a lever against nothing, a pulley, both at once, a ring around the jaw, a split to the nose, or a check from the harness. This is the single largest difference between any two bits, and it is read from the parts, not the name.

2 Choose the cheek

The cheek decides how the rein force reaches the mouthpiece, what stops it, and whether the bit can be pulled through, ridden up, or clamped on the jaw. Filter by where the cheek is used; the mechanics do not change with the discipline.

3 Choose the mouthpiece

The mouthpiece decides how that force is spread over tongue, bars, lips and palate.

The whole matrix at a glance

Every cheek (rows, grouped by mechanism) against every mouthpiece (columns), at the reference condition: 20 N on each rein, head on the vertical, catalogue diameters. The colour is the harm class, which is the worst of three readings: where the peak pressure stands against the pain thresholds, the most serious lesion the model expects, and whether pain is the device's mechanism. The number is the harm index, 0–100, which also weighs the expected injuries by their severity. Click a cell to open its dossier.
