Book III · Standard

Imperial Emergency Medical Carriage Technical Standard

The design, testing and certification of emergency medical carriages, their teams, their routes and their crews.

This Standard shall regulate the technical design, testing, and certification of emergency medical carriages, ambulance teams, emergency lanes, glideways, sleigh-compatible routes, suspension, braking, patient restraint, and equid recovery.

Status This is the final and binding technical standard for emergency medical carriages. It implements the carriage-law duty to provide the fastest safe medical transport and the lowest practicable patient jostle. Cite as the Imperial Emergency Medical Carriage Technical Standard. Three of its sections are headed as reserved; each carries text in the archive and is transcribed below.

Article 1. Core legal duty

Every emergency medical carriage shall be engineered, tested, authorised, and maintained to provide the fastest safe medical transport and the lowest practicable patient jostle consistent with equid welfare, hoof safety, braking safety, route suitability, and patient protection.

A slower or more jostling design shall not be approved where a feasible faster and lower-jostle design exists without greater equid welfare burden.

Article 2. Reserved technical tests

  • route testing;
  • loaded testing;
  • suspension testing;
  • braking testing;
  • patient-jostle testing;
  • stretcher-lock testing;
  • wheel, runner, and glide element testing;
  • hoof-traction testing;
  • relay-system testing;
  • equid recovery testing.

Article 3. Reserved design requirements

  • high-quality suspension;
  • low centre of gravity;
  • secure stretcher locking;
  • patient restraint;
  • medical-equipment restraint;
  • brakes not dependent on equid body strain;
  • route-compatible wheels, runners, or glide elements;
  • hoof-safe emergency lanes;
  • runner or wheel guidance where used;
  • non-slip surfaces;
  • safe crossing design;
  • emergency pull-offs;
  • relay access where necessary;
  • service lighting and markers that do not frighten or blind equids.

Article 4. Reserved ambulance team model

  • Four-equid teams may be used where required by speed, stability, route, or vehicle design.
  • Leaders selected for speed, line confidence, route focus, road courage, and endurance.
  • Wheelers selected for strength, braking stability, turn stability, calmness near the carriage, and smooth power.
  • All emergency equids must be individually certified and recover safely from service demand.

Binding certification standard

Article 5. Vehicle certification

Every emergency medical carriage shall be certified for the actual patient platform, suspension, brakes, wheels or runners, route class, surface, load, team formation, lighting, switchable marker illumination, marker power reserve, and medical equipment carried. Certification of a visually similar vehicle is insufficient.

The vehicle shall have:

  • independent service and parking brakes capable of holding the fully loaded vehicle on the steepest approved gradient without relying on the equids;
  • a low, stable centre of gravity;
  • guarded wheels, runners, doors, and moving parts;
  • secure patient access and egress;
  • a stretcher locking system with at least two independent retention points;
  • restraint for the patient and every loose medical item;
  • suspension and tyres or runners selected for the route;
  • emergency release accessible from both sides;
  • communication with emergency control;
  • lawful lighting, markers, and hazard systems.

Article 6. Braking and stability tests

The fully loaded vehicle shall stop repeatedly from its certified maximum route speed without wheel lock, jack-knifing, shaft impact, uncontrolled runner slide, excessive pitch, or avoidable equid braking strain. The parking brake shall hold for not less than ten minutes on the steepest approved gradient in both travel directions.

The vehicle shall pass loaded turning, camber, obstacle, rut, wet-surface, emergency-lane, and crosswind tests without wheel lift, rollover tendency, dangerous sway, patient-platform instability, or harness impact.

Article 7. Patient-jostle test

Patient-platform vibration and shock shall be measured on every approved route class under full representative load. The vehicle shall not be approved where a feasible suspension, wheel, tyre, runner, route, speed, or loading change would materially reduce patient jostle without increasing equid welfare burden or delaying care.

A technical report shall record root-mean-square vertical acceleration, peak shocks, lateral movement, pitch, roll, test speed, route surface, load, tyre pressure or runner condition, and comparison with alternative configurations. The lowest-jostle lawful configuration shall be used.

Article 8. Speed and routing

Certified maximum speed shall be route-specific and shall account for stopping distance, visibility, surface, gradient, team recovery, patient condition, and vehicle stability. Emergency status does not authorise galloping, unsafe cornering, close passing, or speed beyond the tested configuration.

Emergency control shall select the fastest safe route, not merely the shortest route. Dedicated hoof-safe emergency lanes, glideways, sleigh routes, relay points, and pull-offs shall be used where they reduce time or jostle without increasing equid risk.

Article 9. Team certification

Every equid shall be individually certified for emergency service, the specific hitch role, route class, speed class, vehicle, bitless driving cues, traffic conditions, standing during loading, emergency release, team separation, and post-service recovery.

A four-equid team may be used where needed for speed, stability, braking distribution, or route demand. Team size shall not be increased to disguise an overweight or poorly designed vehicle.

Leaders shall be certified for line confidence, route focus, traffic courage, sustainable speed, and recovery. Wheelers shall be certified for smooth power, braking stability, turn stability, and calmness near the vehicle. Breed does not replace individual certification.

Article 10. Relay system

A relay plan is mandatory where the likely incident route, weather, workload, or return journey may exceed the certified ordinary capacity of one team. Relay equids shall be rested, inspected, immediately available, and not simultaneously assigned elsewhere.

No team shall continue because a relay was unavailable where continuing would exceed lawful capacity. Emergency control shall reroute, transfer the patient to another certified emergency carriage, call a rested relay team, or stabilise and treat the patient at the nearest safe medical point. Motor road transport is not a lawful alternative in Hrossagrið.

Article 11. Crew

The driver shall hold an emergency-medical carriage licence for the team, vehicle, route, speed, and service class. A second competent crew member shall attend the patient compartment unless the service design provides an equally safe alternative.

The crew shall be trained in equid first aid, human first aid appropriate to role, emergency unhitching, vehicle fire, collision response, runaway prevention, scene protection, stretcher handling, communications, and transfer to other transport.

Article 12. Pre-service inspection

Before every duty period, the service shall inspect the equids, harness, headgear, vehicle, brakes, wheels or runners, suspension, stretcher locks, restraints, road lighting, emergency warning systems, markers, marker-illumination switching, brightness, battery reserve and backup, communications, emergency tools, water, and relay readiness. A failed safety item removes the unit from service until corrected.

Article 13. Post-service recovery

After each emergency response, every equid shall receive an immediate recorded welfare check. Any response exceeding ordinary limits, involving high climate burden, collision, slipping, entanglement, abnormal recovery, or distress requires veterinary or competent-authority review and not less than forty-eight hours of recovery leave, with longer leave where ordered.

The vehicle, harness, and equipment shall be inspected after every serious response and before reuse.

Article 14. Incident records

The service shall preserve dispatch time, authorisation, patient-loading time, route, speed, stops, team, relay use, climate, load, vehicle data, welfare observations, arrival time, patient transfer, post-service findings, and any equipment or road failure for at least ten years.

Article 15. Offences

It is an offence to claim emergency status for ordinary transport, use an uncertified team or vehicle, exceed certified speed or route limits, omit a required relay, rely on equids instead of brakes, carry unsecured equipment, falsify response times or welfare records, or continue after an equid or vehicle stop condition.

Article 16. Finality

This Standard is binding emergency-medical carriage law. Later engineering manuals may add detail or stricter requirements but shall not weaken it.
