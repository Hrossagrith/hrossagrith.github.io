Book II · Part I

Health

Free at the point of use, heavily funded, and deliberately designed against the failures that make free healthcare fail elsewhere.

Healthcare in Hrossagrið is free at the point of use. That much is easy to declare and hard to deliver, so the design goal is stated in the negative: the system is built to prevent waiting lists, staff shortages and regional inequality, rather than to announce a right and then ration it quietly.

Evidence before intention

Why the Empire re-tests what it already believes

Ethics decides what counts as constructive; science establishes what is actually happening. A treatment is not adopted because its purpose is good. It is adopted because research, replication, statistics, monitoring of adverse effects and comparison with alternatives say it works. If a drug does not work, good intentions do not license its use.

The same rule applies backwards in time. New research is not automatically better than old research, and an excellent old study that survives modern testing stays. But an old claim earns no permanent immunity from having sat in textbooks for a century. Old medical dogmas, psychological theories and professional standards are asked the same question: was this ever properly tested?

Women and children

Where the historical record is weakest

Much of medical literature was produced in societies with severe sexism and very different attitudes to children. Where the older evidence studied women poorly, the Empire does not treat that as unfortunate but final. It deliberately funds research into women’s pain, gynaecological disease, cardiovascular disease in women, drug effects in women, autism and other conditions in girls, and maternal health.

A child is not treated as a smaller adult. Research has to account for a developing body and mind, and clinical practice follows the research rather than tradition.

In preparation. The organisation of imperial hospitals, veterinary-adjacent services, ambulance provision and the medical professions is not yet written into the register. The Imperial Emergency Medical Carriage Technical Standard, which governs ambulance carriages, is in force.

Related Imperial Emergency Medical Carriage Technical Standard — ambulance speed, low jostle, suspension, emergency lanes and testing.

Principle Ethics says what matters. Science says what is true. Neither substitutes for the other.
