Transport and roads

A country whose road culture is built on equids, and the one narrow exception it makes for motor freight.

Hrossagrið fundamentally does not want ordinary motor roads. Its public roads are built around ridden and driven equids, and every animal entering one carries a road-status marker declaring what other road users must do about it.

Hrossaway

The exception, and why it exists

The Empire also exists in the real world. It needs imports, exports, medicine, industrial equipment, raw materials and international trade. It could refuse all motor freight on principle — and then damage its economy, complicate the supply of medical equipment, and create a great deal of new harm. That would be a strange outcome for a system whose whole purpose is to minimise destruction.

Hrossaway is the resulting narrow exception: a closed motor-freight road running from an international port or border freight point to a specific transfer terminal. A truck enters. It unloads. From there goods continue by rail or by heavy horse-drawn freight teams. The principle is not abandoned, and it is also not turned into a suicidal dogma.

Rail

Long-distance, high-speed and electric — and nothing else

Rail is already written into the register. The Imperial Road and Driver Code limits the Empire’s mechanised public land transport to long-distance high-speed electric rail on dedicated mainline tracks. There is no metro, no underground urban railway, no tramway, no suburban commuter railway, no light rail, no local rapid-transit railway, and no diesel or steam service. Rail carries travel between distant regions and principal settlements; movement inside a settlement, and between a station and the district around it, happens on foot, by ridden or led equid, or by equid-drawn vehicle.

The same Code separates the two networks physically. High-speed railway corridors are kept apart from equid roads, settlements, pastures and public paths, and a public road crosses an active high-speed line only by grade-separated bridge or underpass. No level crossing, road-running rail, shared tramway surface or mixed rail-and-equid corridor is permitted. Stations, access roads, loading areas and service yards are designed so that train noise, air movement, vibration, lighting, barriers, crowds and sudden movement do not create avoidable danger to an equid.

Carriages and emergency transport

Where the binding detail lives

Carriage work is governed in full by the Imperial Code for the Welfare of Working Carriage Equids: passports, certification, workload and rest, weather burden, route safety, harness, headgear mounts, road-status inserts, lighting and ground-guidance lamps. Ambulance carriages have their own technical standard covering speed, low jostle, suspension, emergency lanes and glideways.

In preparation. Road authority and infrastructure law — construction, maintenance and control of the public road network — is reserved for separate drafting so that carriage welfare law does not absorb it. The wider public-infrastructure system beyond the road and rail rules set out above is not yet written into the register.

On the roads Every equid on a public road wears approved bitless headgear with a Facial Marker.
