The founding meaning

Hrossagrið is not named for its horses. It is named for its grið — guaranteed peace, safe conduct, quarter, sanctuary — and for the horses that define whom the sanctuary is owed to.

The name began as an exonym. Merchants, messengers, refugees and riders crossing the border found that pursuit had to stop: the traveller, their companions and their equids all entered protection. Horses could not be seized for war, stolen, requisitioned, traded away, or used as leverage against the people who depended on them. Outsiders called that condition hrossagrið, and in time the word travelled outward from the border to the roads, the settlements, the recognised territory, and finally to the country itself.

Protection of a person is incomplete where the person’s horse, means of movement, livelihood, or route home may still be seized, abused, exhausted, or used as leverage.

What the Empire is

Modern Hrossagrið is a human nation, and a highly diverse one: its population descends from numerous peoples integrated over centuries through migration and refuge. Horses and other equids are protected living beings, companions and working partners, and the foundation of the Empire’s road-transport culture. They are not the population, and they hold no human political office.

Constitutionally, the Empire is a eudaimonocratic diarchic monarchy: two co-equal Crowns, the Guiding and the Guarding, governing under a philosophy that measures a policy by what it builds and what it destroys across the whole breadth of life rather than by what a majority happens to want. The equid law the Empire is known for abroad is not a curiosity attached to that system. It follows from it, and from the name.

Use the international th-based forms wherever the letter ð is unavailable, unfamiliar, or difficult to type.

Historical truth. The Empire does not rewrite, erase, or sanitise its history for political purposes. Its record of its own successes, failures, injustices and controversial decisions is meant to be accurate, and the history section states the limits of its own reconstruction rather than hiding them.
